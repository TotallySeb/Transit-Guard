# The History of LA Metro

## Introduction

The Los Angeles County Metropolitan Transportation Authority, commonly known as **LA Metro**, is the agency responsible for planning, constructing, and operating public transportation in Los Angeles County, California. The Metro system includes buses, light rail, and subway lines that serve millions of riders annually.

---

## Early Beginnings

- **1980 – Formation of LA Metro**  
  LA Metro was established in 1980 through the consolidation of several transit agencies to better coordinate transit services in Los Angeles County.

- **1986 – Opening of the Blue Line (A Line)**  
  The first modern light rail line, connecting Downtown Los Angeles to Long Beach, opened in 1990 (originally called the Blue Line; now the A Line).

- **1993 – Red Line Subway Opens**  
  The first segment of the Red Line subway (now part of the B and D Lines) began operation, marking the return of heavy rail subway service to LA after decades.

---

## Expansion and Growth

- **2000s – Metro Rail Expansion**  
  LA Metro expanded its light rail and subway lines rapidly:
  - The Gold Line (now the L Line) opened in 2003 connecting Pasadena to Downtown LA.
  - The Green Line (C Line) light rail opened in 1995, serving the south LA area and LAX vicinity.
  - Continued extensions and improvements on the Red and Blue Lines.

- **2010s – Measure R and Measure M**  
  Voters approved sales tax measures funding billions for transit expansion, including:
  - The Expo Line extension (now E Line) connecting Downtown LA to Santa Monica.
  - The Crenshaw/LAX Line (now K Line).
  - Extensions of the Purple Line subway westward.

---

## Recent Developments and Future Plans

- **2020s – New Projects Underway**  
  - Purple Line Extension: Extending the subway west toward Westwood and UCLA.
  - Regional Connector Transit Project: Linking the A, E, and L Lines for seamless travel through Downtown LA.
  - Automated people movers at LAX to improve airport connectivity.

- **Sustainability Focus**  
  LA Metro is working towards zero-emission buses and sustainable infrastructure.

---

## Impact of LA Metro

- Provided alternatives to car travel in one of America’s most car-dependent regions.
- Enabled economic development and improved mobility across LA County.
- Supports major events and daily commutes for millions.
