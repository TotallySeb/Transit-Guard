# Safety Concerns on the Metro Purple Line (D Line)

The Metro Purple Line (now officially called the D Line) runs between **Union Station** and **Wilshire/Western**, serving dense urban corridors including Koreatown and Downtown LA. While less publicized than other lines, the Purple Line faces **serious safety issues**, particularly related to **crime spillover from downtown**, **underground station conditions**, and **homelessness**.

---

## 🚨 Key Safety Concerns

### 1. Crime Spillover from Downtown LA
- Stations like **Pershing Square**, **Civic Center/Grand Park**, and **Westlake/MacArthur Park** are common locations for **theft, assault, and aggressive panhandling**.
- Riders have reported repeated incidents of **phone snatching, harassment, and groping**, especially during rush hour.
- Some stations are located in **high-crime areas**, with limited visibility and no dedicated security presence.

### 2. Underground Isolation and Poor Lighting
- As a **fully underground line**, many Purple Line stations feel claustrophobic and isolated.
- **Dim lighting**, **dead zones for cell reception**, and **poor camera visibility** make riders vulnerable during emergencies.
- Reports of **fights, mental health crises, and drug activity** in underground corridors go unmonitored.

### 3. Drug Use and Unhoused Populations
- Many riders report **people sleeping or using drugs** on train cars and in station lobbies.
- **Lack of shelter space** and the underground nature of stations have turned the line into a de facto refuge during extreme weather.
- Encampments or individuals in crisis are frequently encountered near **Wilshire/Normandie** and **Wilshire/Vermont** stations.

### 4. Delayed Police and Metro Responses
- The **response time from Metro security** or LAPD is often inadequate.
- Emergency intercoms are not always functional or are ignored.
- Multiple riders have stated they **stopped filing reports** due to lack of follow-up.

## 📉 Impact on Ridership

- Despite its usefulness for K-Town and downtown commuters, many avoid the Purple Line **due to safety concerns**, especially women and elderly riders.
- Businesses along Wilshire Blvd report fewer customers willing to take Metro due to its **“unsafe and filthy” reputation**.
- Surveys in 2023 found that **nearly 40% of former D Line riders stopped using the service** due to safety fears.

---

## 🛠 Recommended Solutions

- Increase **visible, uniformed patrols** on trains and at stations during peak hours.
- Add **mental health outreach workers** to respond to crises compassionately.
- Upgrade **station lighting, emergency phones, and surveillance systems**.
- Publicly share safety statistics and incident reports to **rebuild rider trust**.
